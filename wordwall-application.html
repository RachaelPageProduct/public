How I'd work at Wordwall

The way I'd approach this role.

Not starting from blank. Starting from understanding how teachers actually use Wordwall, where the current design system holds up, and where evidence — not opinion — should settle a debate.

01

Get inside the product and the classroom use case fast

Before proposing anything, I'd spend the first stretch using Wordwall as a teacher would, reviewing existing research and analytics, and talking to anyone on the team who knows where real friction lives. Millions of active users means existing signal is probably underused — I'd start there before generating new opinions.

02

Reframe ambiguous asks into testable design problems

When a request arrives half-formed — "make this more engaging," "teachers are dropping off here" — I turn it into a specific, falsifiable question and a lightweight way to answer it, whether that's a usability test, a split test, or a quick round of qualitative interviews. Evidence settles the debate, not seniority.

03

Treat the design system as shared infrastructure

I'd work with whatever exists in Figma today, standardise it where it's inconsistent, and document it so PMs and engineers can self-serve rather than waiting on design. A system only earns its keep if it's actively maintained — I've done that for 26+ months straight on a previous product, not as a side project.

04

Raise the bar through critique, not just my own output

Four years of formal mentoring means I default to sharing reasoning, not just polished outcomes — in critique, in Figma comments, in how I frame decisions to PMs and engineers. In a lean, remote team, raising the team's craft matters as much as my own delivery.
